The README

I wouldn't make it long. Something like:

RAGBRAI Rider Companion

An open-source, offline-first guide for self-supported cyclists participating in RAGBRAI.

The project combines official route data with rider-focused logistics including groceries, laundromats, pharmacies, bike shops, Garmin POIs, historical notes, and local businesses.

Designed to function without cellular service during ride week.

That tells visitors exactly what the project is and who it's for.

I also have an idea for the repository philosophy.

Every recommendation should answer one question:

"Would I stop here if I were riding today?"

If the answer is yes, it belongs in the Companion.

If not, we don't add it just to make the guide bigger.

That keeps the guide curated rather than becoming an overwhelming directory.

Where I think this could go

I genuinely think this has the potential to become a respected community resource. Not because it tries to compete with the official RAGBRAI information, but because it serves a different purpose.

The official route materials are about the event.

The RAGBRAI Rider Companion is about the rider.

That's an important distinction.

For example:

The official guide tells you where the overnight town is.
The Companion tells you which grocery store has the shortest walk from the route, where the laundromat is, where to buy chain lube, and which local café is worth a five-minute detour.

Those are complementary resources, not competing ones.

One final suggestion

Let's adopt a simple mantra for the project:

"Ride prepared. Ride local. Ride offline."

It captures the three principles we've kept coming back to:

Prepared: anticipate needs before they become problems.
Local: encourage riders to support Iowa's small businesses and communities.
Offline: ensure the Companion remains useful even when cell service is saturated.

I'm genuinely excited about this project. It combines route analysis, mapping, software, documentation, and real-world cycling into something that doesn't really exist today. I think by the time we publish the first complete edition, it will be a resource that many self-supported riders—and even some supported riders—will want to download before heading to Iowa.
